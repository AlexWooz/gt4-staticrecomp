## GSdx study, second question: where their GS thread's CPU goes (TASK-216)

Read-only, docs only, same worktree and branch as yesterday. No build, no run, no code/pin/test/data change. The trigger: the
GS worker is now the measured bottleneck (89-98 % of the frame in every scene, busy not waiting; TASK-215
`docs/tasks/COREGS2-PIPELINE-SPLIT.md`), and the owner says GSdx still runs better on weak devices.

New page `docs/gsdx/GS-THREAD-CPU-COST.md` (linked from the study and the README); `TASKS.md` TASK-216 and `STATUS.md` updated.

### What it says

- **Their GS is two threads on Vulkan.** MTGS parses GIF and kicks vertices; a back thread runs the draw path from a ring of
  self-contained records (state snapshot + vertex/index spans, pooled buffers). Present and merge stay on MTGS behind a drain.
  Refused on non-Vulkan and for downloads that read live memory. On the S23 the private build defaults to OpenGL, where the
  split is off, so the owner's comparison may not even include it (Unknown, carried from the first study).
- **The per-vertex path is one fused, SIMD pass.** A recognised packed layout is walked as a run: pass one decodes into a side
  table, pass two writes vertices **and indices** with the trace folded in, the cull uses packed band outcodes and a per-buffer
  mirror refreshed on scissor changes. No per-vertex call, no per-primitive record, no allocation.
- **No CPU vertex translate.** They keep the guest's 12.4 words and the vertex shader converts; we convert every vertex to
  floats in `TranslateBatch` (0.45 ms on the license).
- **One command buffer per frame, submitted at present.** Extra submits only for a readback (submit + wait), a stream-buffer
  spill, an allocation failure and the armed kick. Per-draw state is a dirty-flag vector: an unchanged state re-applies
  nothing (one load and a branch).
- **Nothing says their GS thread is lighter on the same CPU.** Their per-draw validation (`Draw()` + `DrawPrims`) is heavier
  than ours in code, the scene work per draw is the same, our whole renderer is 9.0 %/11.4 % faster at 3x/12x on the desktop,
  and the black-box run has the game stalling 4.263 ms/frame waiting for their GS process. The measured weak-device cause is
  the GPU side (11.5 ms of GPU at both 1x and 2x on the S23 with ~750 fragment barriers, 75 full barriers and 20 passes).

### Our numbers it compares against (C)

License B-1 1x: 1,930 batches, 9,197 draws, 26.5k primitives, 1,553 CLUT loads, 145.6 uploads (3.6 MB swizzled), ~50 decodes
per VBlank. Stages: gif_parse 3.36, batch_own 0.63, draw 0.94, clut 0.48, swizzle 0.48, translate 0.45, texture 0.19,
readback 0.08, GS total 7.67. The post-front-end total is 2.85 ms across the ~500 real draw batches (~5.7 us each; the 1,430
CLUT-flush batches are ~0.1 us each, IN-554), so the "2.85 ms per draw" is per VBlank, not per draw.

### Adoptable, ranked (bound / gate)

1. Front end and draw side on two threads, ring of self-contained records: TASK-215's own 1.5-3 ms (H), faithful, frames
   identical; their back thread is the existence proof.
2. Batched strip decode and kick (their kernel's shape, scalar first): 0.5-1.0 ms (Hyp), faithful, differential fixture
   replaying a tag vertex by vertex.
3. No CPU translate (device-format vertices, shader conversion): 0.2-0.4 ms (Hyp), faithful, CPU reference stays the oracle.
4. Index-at-kick instead of per-primitive records (scissor only when it changes; IN-542 measured ~1 change/VBlank): small,
   exactness risk on per-primitive scissors.
5. Android: verify the ring memory type (we pick HOST_CACHED on integrated) and use the release branch's K rule for the submit
   cadence (S23: `SUBMIT_EVERY=128` was +11.6 % VBlanks/s, 32 % fewer passes).

### Unknowns

Their GS thread's CPU on our recordings (the premise itself); the real cost of their per-draw validation; how much of our
gif_parse a batched kick removes in practice; whether our Android rings land in a cached coherent type; their SIMD trace
against our scalar `ComputeVertexTrace`; the pass scheduler's per-draw flush-check cost.
