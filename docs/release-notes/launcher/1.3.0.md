# GT4Recomp 1.3.0

**CoreGS 2.0, our own renderer, in beta; Gran Turismo 4 Spec II 1.10; RetroAchievements for Gran Turismo 4; 7 controller response curves and standardized trigger travel.**

## New
- CoreGS 2.0 (beta), our own renderer for Gran Turismo 4: choose it in Settings > Graphics. In our tests it uses about half the processor of GSdx, while GSdx still uses a little less graphics card.
- The renderer decides which graphics options you see. CoreGS 2.0 has FSR1 sharpening instead of CAS, and always draws with Vulkan, so the API option disappears.
- Performance Mode on CoreGS 2.0: off draws with full graphics precision; on turns off the night lights, the sun glare and the sheen on the road and draws small effects at lower resolution for more speed.
- Frame rate choice on CoreGS 2.0, experimental, 60 FPS by default: at 30, races run at a steady 30 frames per second, for PCs that cannot hold 60, at full speed and with the same physics. Menus stay at 60. In our test at 12x on an RX 9060 XT: full speed with each frame within 1 ms of the last, against 77% speed at 46 at the 60 setting.
- Performance HUD on CoreGS 2.0, off by default (Settings > Graphics > Advanced): frame rate, frame time with a live graph, and the game's use of the processor, graphics card and memory.
- Every option in Settings is now chosen with arrows, as on the home screen, and goes round without an end: easier with a controller.
- Custom textures work on CoreGS 2.0 too: existing texture packs in the Textures folder load unchanged.
- Gran Turismo 4 Spec II 1.10 joins Gran Turismo 4: pick the game on the home screen; each game keeps its own disc, saves and replays.
- RetroAchievements support for Gran Turismo 4: sign in with your username and password in Settings > Achievements to track achievements and rich presence.
- 7 controller response curves in Settings > Controls (linear, quadratic, cubic, s-curve, smooth power, sublinear, logarithmic) to adjust steering and trigger sensitivity.
- Standardized trigger travel across gamepads for consistent acceleration and braking range.
- One Saves list for both games, each save tagged with its game, plus New save, Export and Rename.
- The launcher picks the graphics API and the best graphics card by itself (for example the NVIDIA card instead of integrated graphics on laptops); both can be changed in Settings > Graphics.
- The game no longer needs Vulkan: on older graphics cards it starts with Direct3D 11.
- You can turn off the game window's frame (borderless fullscreen) in Settings > Graphics > Display mode, and pick which monitor it starts on.
- The launcher finds and prepares your disc faster.

## Fixes
- On some Intel graphics cards Vulkan lost the video card when entering the showroom; the launcher now switches to Direct3D 11 and remembers it for the next time.
- The launcher no longer says the game crashed when you close it normally.
- The Render scale slider now goes up one step at a time: 100% is the automatic size for your screen, and each step above is one notch sharper (before, 125% could jump several steps at once).
- On integrated graphics the game starts at native resolution, and importing PCSX2 memory cards (8 to 64 MB) no longer closes the launcher.

## Performance
- CoreGS 2.0 asks less of the processor: racing on George V Paris at 6x, about 8% less processor work per frame in the graphics thread, 7% in Performance Mode (automated runs on an RX 9060 XT).
- Less input lag on CoreGS 2.0 on a 60 Hz display with vsync: the time from a finished picture to the screen falls from about 53 ms to about 40 ms (automated runs on an RX 9060 XT).
- A heavy stretch no longer costs frame rate on CoreGS 2.0 with Performance Mode off: the game stops waiting for the graphics thread at the light flares, about 9.5 ms per frame on George V Paris at 6x, with the same picture.
- The drop as the cars roll to the line at a race start is gone on CoreGS 2.0: in that stretch at 6x the slowest 5% of frames go from about 20 ms to about 13 ms (automated runs on an RX 9060 XT).
- Less waiting for the graphics card on CoreGS 2.0 with vsync on: racing on George V Paris at 6x, frames over 20 ms fall from 29 to 1 in 1,000, with no Performance Mode (automated runs on an RX 9060 XT).
- Steadier frame times on CoreGS 2.0: on George V Paris at 6x the change from one frame to the next drops from about 4 ms to about 0.4 ms, and the slowest 5% of frames from about 22 ms to about 15 ms (about 17 ms to about 13 ms in Performance Mode), with the same picture (automated runs on an RX 9060 XT).
- CoreGS 2.0 stops waiting for the graphics card when a texture lands next to a picture being drawn: about 17% less time per frame on George V Paris at 6x, about 20% in Performance Mode, with the same picture (automated runs on an RX 9060 XT).
- CoreGS 2.0 is lighter on weak graphics: videos behind the menus play smoothly, about 25% less graphics work at 2x on an Intel HD 620 (about 33% in Performance Mode), about 8% less at 12x on an RX 9060 XT, with the same picture.
- CoreGS 2.0 compiles its shaders before the game starts, in a window after you press Play. This happens the first time and after an update or a new graphics driver; otherwise the game starts directly.

## Good to know
- GSdx stays the default renderer. CoreGS 2.0 runs both games and needs a graphics card with Vulkan 1.2.
- Achievements are active for Gran Turismo 4 (v2.00) and isolated from Spec II playthroughs.
- Stored RetroAchievements session tokens are protected with Windows Data Protection (DPAPI). If you move a portable folder to another PC or Windows account, simply sign in again to reconnect.
- Gran Turismo 4 Spec II is a mod by TheAdmiester and contributors; full credits in About. GT4Recomp ships no Spec II files: you need your own Spec II 1.10 disc image.
- Your saves and settings are kept when you update. The game program is now called gt4.exe (Spec II: gt4-spec2.exe).
