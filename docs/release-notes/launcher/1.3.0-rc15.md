# GT4Recomp 1.3.0-rc15

**Racing wheels work natively: the game sees a Logitech Driving Force Pro with its own force feedback, set up on a new
Wheel page. Also a choice of performance HUD, smoother play on the Steam Deck and fewer shader crashes.**

## New
- Racing wheels: the game now sees your wheel as a Logitech Driving Force Pro, with 900 degrees of steering and the game's own
  force feedback on your wheel.
- Settings > Wheel: the on-screen wheel turns with yours and the pedals show their travel. A Logitech G27 starts from a
  ready mapping (the buttons of your own wheel, nothing guessed); any other wheel is mapped in a short guided setup. Pedal
  dead zones, force strength and a Test force button.
- Mapping is one click: click the control on the wheel, then press the button, turn the wheel or press the pedal; Cancel and
  Clear are right there. The wheel goes into the game by itself whenever it is connected, so there is nothing to switch on.
- Performance HUD: choose Off, FPS only or Full. Full shows your graphics card and processor names in full, and whether the
  performance report is recording.
- Settings > Wheel: a Centring spring row (Off by default, or Follow the game) chooses whether the game's own menu spring is
  played.
- Steam Deck and other graphics cards with a single graphics queue: the picture is shown on its own thread, so waiting for the
  screen no longer holds up the game (Settings > Graphics, on by default).
- The old Performance Mode is now individual switches on Settings > Graphics, all on by default: sun glare and light flares,
  the shine on the road, how big small effects are drawn, and, with GSdx, blending accuracy and readbacks. Turning off only
  what costs you frames in your PC is up to you now; a stored Performance Mode keeps its effect (every switch off).

## Fixes
- The wheel no longer slowly pulls itself back to the centre on its own. GT4 asks for a centring spring in its menus; it is
  off now, and the car, the kerbs, the bumps and your strength setting are unchanged.
- The force feedback now behaves like the reference emulator: the wheel's own "default spring" command is ignored, the game's
  menu spring really stops when the game stops it, and each side of the friction uses its own strength. The damping starts at
  0%.
- Choice rows now change only through their arrows: tap or click the left arrow to go back and the right arrow to go forward.
  Tapping the name or the value no longer changes anything, so a tap next to an arrow never goes the wrong way.
- The arrows are much easier to hit with a finger, and the row looks exactly as before.
- A long setting name no longer breaks in the middle of a word.
- If the graphics driver closes the game while CoreGS 2.0 prepares its shaders, the launcher now tries again by itself and
  shows the attempt; a shader is left out only after it fails twice.
- CoreGS 2.0 now turns on every graphics card feature it uses, and no longer refuses cards that lack formats it never needs.
- Deleting a save now really deletes it: on a PC whose game is on one drive and Windows on another, the save came back at the
  next start. New save works there too, instead of stopping with "cannot move the file to a different disk drive".

## Performance
- A little less processor work in races: about 0.3 ms per frame at 1x on a recorded Paris lap (RTX 3060 laptop).

## Good to know
- This is a test build to try racing wheels. So far the wheel has been tested on one setup; tell us how yours behaves.
- Exported logs now include a fuller system report (processor, memory, Windows or Proton, graphics driver, screens,
  controllers and wheels, with no names or serial numbers) and your wheel settings.
